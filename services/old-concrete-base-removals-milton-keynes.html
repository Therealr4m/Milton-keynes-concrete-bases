Replacing Concrete Bases Milton Keynes

An old concrete base can look acceptable from a distance while the problems underneath are already affecting the structure above it. Cracks, movement, low spots and sinking edges are all signs that the existing installation may no longer be doing its job.

Our approach is to remove the old base rather than build around a problem that is already there. We break out the existing concrete, clear the area and inspect what is underneath before preparing the replacement.

This is particularly important in established gardens where old slabs, partial concrete bases and previous structures may have been left underneath the current installation.

The replacement base is then planned around the actual ground conditions, the dimensions required and the access available to the property.

Removing The Old Base Properly

Old concrete can be substantial once it has been in place for years. We organise the removal so the existing slab, broken sections and unsuitable material are taken out before the replacement base is prepared.

Breaking Out Existing Concrete

The old base is broken up and removed from the working area. Where previous slabs or partial bases have been built at different levels, we deal with the affected sections rather than simply leaving weak material underneath the new installation.

Checking What Is Underneath

Once the old concrete has been removed, the ground can be assessed properly. This is where we can identify soft areas, disturbed ground, old hardcore or other material that could contribute to future movement.

Preparing The Replacement Platform

The replacement area is excavated and brought to the appropriate level before a suitable sub-base is installed and compacted. The objective is to create a stable platform rather than hide the original problem underneath fresh concrete.

When An Existing Concrete Base Needs Replacing

We commonly deal with replacement bases where an existing slab has reached the end of its useful life or was never prepared properly in the first place.

A Shed Base That Has Started Sinking

If one side of a shed has dropped or the concrete has developed an uneven surface, the cause may be below the slab. Removing the old base allows us to deal with the ground and sub-base instead of installing another slab over unstable material.

A Cracked Base For A Garden Office

Repeated cracking can make an existing base unsuitable for a replacement garden office or similar structure. Before installing the new base, we remove the damaged concrete and establish what caused the movement.

An Old Partial Base

Some gardens have several generations of paving and concrete beneath an existing structure. Where old slabs or sections of concrete are mixed with areas of soil, the replacement needs to be prepared consistently rather than bridging between different materials.

Replacing A Base Before A New Structure

If an old shed, cabin or outbuilding is being removed and replaced, it is often the right time to deal with the base as well. We can assess the existing footprint and prepare the new installation around the structure you are putting back.

Ground Preparation Is Where The Replacement Starts

Removing the old concrete is only part of the job. Once the slab is out, we need to make sure the ground beneath it is suitable for the replacement base.

Gardens with old slabs and partial bases can contain a mixture of compacted material, loose fill, soil and previous hardcore. We remove unsuitable material where required and establish a consistent formation for the new base.

A suitable MOT Type 1 sub-base is then installed and compacted where the project requires it. Proper compaction helps prevent the new concrete from following the same movement that caused the original base to fail.

We also consider finished levels and drainage. The base needs to shed water appropriately without creating a situation where rainwater collects against the structure or surrounding ground.

Restricted Access Is Planned Before Removal Starts

Removing an old base can create more logistics than installing a new one, particularly when the garden has limited access from the road or side of the property.

We consider how broken concrete and excavation spoil will be moved out, as well as how the replacement sub-base and fresh concrete will reach the working area.

Working Around Narrow Garden Access

Where there is no straightforward route for equipment or concrete delivery, we organise the work around the available access. The removal and replacement stages are planned together so the site does not become unnecessarily congested.

Concrete Supply And Delivery

For the replacement pour, concrete supply is arranged around the dimensions of the new base and the practical conditions at the property. Local batching and delivery options are considered before the pour rather than left until the last minute.

This matters when access is restricted because the delivery method, timing and distance from the vehicle to the base all affect how efficiently fresh concrete can be placed.

Our Old Concrete Base Removal Process

We keep replacement work organised from the first site visit through to the new concrete being finished. The exact method depends on the existing slab, ground conditions, footprint and access.

1. Site Assessment

We look at the existing base, what is being built on the replacement and how the garden can be accessed. We also identify existing slabs, partial bases, changes in level and any obvious drainage considerations.

2. Setting Out The Removal Area

The existing installation is assessed against the required replacement footprint. This gives us a clear working area and helps avoid removing more of the garden than necessary.

3. Breaking Out And Removing The Old Concrete

The existing concrete is broken up and removed. Old slabs, loose sections and unsuitable material beneath the original base are dealt with where necessary.

4. Excavation And Ground Preparation

The exposed ground is brought to the required level. Soft or unsuitable material is removed where needed before the replacement sub-base is installed.

5. Sub-Base Compaction

MOT Type 1 or other appropriate sub-base material is placed and compacted to form a stable platform. This stage is particularly important where the original base failed because of movement beneath the concrete.

6. Formwork, Levels And Reinforcement

Formwork is set out to the required dimensions and finished levels. Reinforcement is included where the requirements of the replacement base make it appropriate.

7. Concrete Placement And Finishing

Fresh concrete is delivered and placed into the prepared area. We level and finish the concrete to suit the intended structure and leave the installation ready for the next stage of the project.

What Can Go Wrong If The Old Base Is Not Removed Properly?

The biggest mistake we see with replacement work is treating the concrete itself as the only problem. If a new slab is simply laid over unstable ground or an old failed base, the underlying movement has not actually been fixed.

Poor Sub-Base Preparation Can Cause Sinking

If the material underneath the replacement is loose, inconsistent or poorly compacted, the new concrete can settle unevenly. Once a shed, office or cabin is sitting on top, correcting that movement becomes far more disruptive.

Uneven Ground Can Create Cracking

Concrete needs a stable platform beneath it. If different sections of the old installation remain underneath the replacement, the base can behave differently across its footprint and develop cracks or uneven areas.

Incorrect Levels Can Create Drainage Problems

A replacement base also needs to work with the surrounding garden. Poorly planned levels can leave water sitting against the base or moving towards the structure instead of being directed away from it.

Doing the preparation properly at the beginning costs less than removing another failed base later. That is why we deal with the underlying installation rather than taking shortcuts to get fresh concrete down quickly.

Why Getting The Replacement Right First Time Saves Money

When we remove an old concrete base, the aim is not simply to leave you with a fresh-looking slab. We want the replacement to be built on ground that has been properly prepared for the structure going on top.

That means taking the time to remove unsuitable material, compact the sub-base, establish sensible levels and organise the concrete placement around the property.

  • We remove the underlying problem rather than covering it
  • Ground and sub-base preparation are dealt with before concrete
  • Levels and drainage are considered during the installation
  • Access and concrete delivery are planned before the pour
  • The replacement is sized around the structure being installed
  • We keep communication direct from assessment through completion

It is much easier to spend the money once on proper preparation than to pay for another removal and replacement because the first installation was rushed.

A Replacement Base Built Around The Actual Property

There is no single removal method that suits every Milton Keynes garden. Some existing bases are relatively straightforward to break out, while others have been built over older paving, raised sections or several layers of previous ground work.

We assess the actual site before deciding how the removal and replacement should be organised. This includes the existing footprint, surrounding levels, ground beneath the old base and the route available for removing waste.

The replacement is then planned around the structure, the finished levels and the practical access available on the day of the concrete pour.
