Concrete Base Installation Buckingham

Many of the properties we work around Buckingham are established semi-detached homes where the garden has changed over time. Old slabs, partial concrete areas, paving and other previous work can sometimes be found beneath or around the proposed position of a new base.

Before installation, we look at what is already there and how the new base will fit into the garden. The ground needs to be prepared properly rather than simply placing concrete over an existing surface that may not provide the right foundation.

Access is also considered from the beginning. Steps, fences, raised sections and restricted side routes can affect how excavation spoil, materials and concrete reach the working area.

If you already know the approximate dimensions of the base, you can contact our concrete base team with the measurements and details of the project so we can discuss the work with you.

Concrete Base Work Planned Around The Property

The installation is organised around the actual garden rather than assuming every Buckingham property can be approached in the same way. Established residential areas can have different side access arrangements, garden levels and existing surfaces.

Established Buckingham Gardens

Older gardens can contain previous slabs, paths or sections of concrete that need to be removed before the new base is prepared. We assess what is present and determine what needs to be cleared before setting out the installation.

Working Around Restricted Access

Some properties have a straightforward route into the garden, while others involve steps, fences or raised sections. These obstacles affect how the excavation and material movement are organised, so they are considered before the work begins.

Concrete Delivery And Site Planning

The route into the garden and the position of the base are considered alongside the concrete delivery. Planning this beforehand helps keep the installation organised when access to the work area is limited.

Every Buckingham Garden Needs A Slightly Different Approach

Two properties can have similar garden dimensions but require completely different preparation. One may have clear side access and suitable ground, while another may have old slabs, uneven levels or obstacles between the property and the proposed base.

We look at these practical details before deciding how the installation should be carried out. The position of the base, existing ground and access route all influence the preparation required.

This matters because the finished concrete is only as reliable as the ground supporting it. A poorly prepared sub-base can contribute to movement or sinking after the installation has been completed.

Preparing The Ground Before The Concrete Is Installed

The preparation stage is where much of the practical work takes place. The installation area is marked out and existing material is removed where necessary to create the space required for the base.

Where the ground requires it, a suitable MOT Type 1 sub-base is placed and compacted before the concrete is installed. The amount of preparation depends on the existing ground and what the base needs to support.

Formwork is then set out to the required dimensions and reinforcement is used where the project calls for it. Fresh concrete is placed, levelled and finished once the prepared area is ready.

The aim is to prepare the ground properly from the start rather than relying on the concrete itself to compensate for poor conditions underneath it.

A Concrete Base Designed Around The Intended Building

The dimensions and position of the base need to work with the building that will eventually sit on it. We discuss the proposed footprint and available garden space before the installation is set out.

Existing Gardens And Previous Surfaces

Where an old slab or partial base is already present, it is important to establish what can remain and what needs to be removed. The new installation is then planned around the actual condition of the site.

Access From The Property To The Work Area

Steps, fences and raised sections can make a short route into the garden more involved. We take these conditions into account when planning excavation, material movement and concrete placement.
