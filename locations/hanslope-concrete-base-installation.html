Why The Installation Approach Matters In Hanslope

A concrete base needs to be prepared for the actual conditions beneath it. Simply placing concrete over an unsuitable or poorly prepared area can create problems later, particularly where the underlying ground has previously been disturbed.

A properly prepared sub-base helps provide the foundation the concrete needs and reduces the risk of movement or sinking. The amount of preparation required depends on what is found on site and what the finished base is intended to support.

This is particularly relevant on established properties where gardens may have been altered over the years. Old paving, previous structures, uneven ground and changes in garden levels can all affect how the installation needs to be approached.

  • Site preparation based on the actual ground
  • Access considered before work begins
  • Base dimensions set around the intended building
  • Practical planning for established gardens
  • Excavation and sub-base preparation carried out as required
  • Concrete placement organised around site access

Access Matters On Hanslope Concrete Base Installations

Access is one of the first practical points we look at when assessing a garden base. A property can have enough garden space for the proposed building, but the route into that space may include a fence, steps, a raised section or a narrow working area.

These conditions affect how excavation spoil is moved away from the work area and how the materials required for the installation are brought into position. Planning this properly helps the job run around the property rather than creating unnecessary disruption.

Older residential layouts can also have garden changes that are not obvious from the initial view of the property. A previous patio, removed structure or altered level may mean the ground beneath the proposed base needs additional preparation.

Where access is restricted, we take the available route into account when organising the work. The objective is to establish how the base can be installed on the actual property before the job gets underway.

Our Hanslope Concrete Base Installation Process

We keep the installation process straightforward while allowing for the differences between individual properties. The exact preparation depends on the proposed base, the existing ground and how easily the work area can be reached.

Initial Site Assessment

We discuss the proposed building and the size of the base required, then consider where it will sit within the garden. Access, existing surfaces, changes in level and any obvious ground issues are considered before the work is planned.

Setting Out And Excavation

The base area is marked out and the existing surface is excavated as required. Unsuitable material and excavation spoil are dealt with as part of preparing the area for the next stage.

Sub-Base Preparation

Where required, MOT Type 1 is installed and compacted to form the prepared layer beneath the concrete. The preparation is based on the existing ground and the requirements of the installation rather than applying the same approach to every garden.

Formwork And Reinforcement

Timber formwork is positioned to establish the required dimensions. Where the project calls for reinforcement, this is placed before the concrete is poured.

Concrete Placement And Finishing

The concrete is placed into the prepared area, levelled and finished to provide the required surface for the intended building. The installation is then checked before the work is completed.

The Base Needs To Work With The Building And The Garden

The position and dimensions of a concrete base need to work with both the intended building and the available garden space. On an established Hanslope property, that can mean considering existing fences, garden levels and the route available for the installation.

Preparation That Helps Prevent Future Problems

Poor preparation beneath a concrete base can contribute to movement or sinking later. We therefore look at the condition of the existing ground and prepare the area according to what the site actually requires.

Whether you are replacing an older base or preparing an established garden for a new outdoor building, the work is organised around the property rather than using a standard approach regardless of the site.
