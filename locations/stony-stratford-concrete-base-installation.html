Stony Stratford Concrete Base Installation

Many of the properties we work around Stony Stratford are modern homes where the garden has been landscaped as part of a newer development. The garden may look finished, but newly laid ground and landscaping can still need proper preparation before a concrete base is installed.

We look at the proposed position of the base, the condition of the ground and the route available from the property to the work area before the installation is organised.

New-build gardens can also have limited entry points. A side gate may be narrow or the route into the rear garden may not allow equipment and materials to be brought straight through. These details are considered before excavation starts rather than dealt with once the job is underway.

Concrete Base Work Planned Around The Property

The garden layout can have a direct effect on how the installation is carried out. Around modern housing developments in Stony Stratford, the available route into the garden can be more restricted than the amount of open garden space suggests.

Modern Housing Layouts

Newer homes often have defined side access and established boundaries between neighbouring properties. Where the route is limited, excavation spoil, sub-base materials and concrete all need to be planned around the access that is actually available.

Recently Landscaped Ground

New gardens can contain recently placed soil, turf and landscaping that has not had much time to settle. Before laying the base, we assess what is beneath the proposed footprint and prepare the area to suit the installation rather than simply laying concrete over the existing surface.

Keeping the work organised helps reduce unnecessary movement around the property and keeps the installation focused on the area being prepared.

Preparing The Ground Properly In Stony Stratford

The preparation beneath a concrete base is one of the main parts of the installation. If the ground below the base is not prepared properly, movement can develop later and the finished structure can be affected.

This is particularly relevant where a garden has recently been landscaped. We remove unsuitable material from the base area and prepare the ground before the concrete is placed.

A suitable MOT Type 1 sub-base is used where required and compacted to create the prepared platform beneath the concrete. The amount of preparation depends on the ground encountered and what the intended building requires.

The aim is not to treat every garden in exactly the same way. The installation is planned around the actual ground and access conditions at the property.

Our Stony Stratford Concrete Base Installation Process

We keep the installation straightforward and organise the work around the property, the available access and the dimensions of the proposed base.

Initial Site Assessment

We discuss the project, look at the proposed position and consider how the work area can be reached. Any narrow gates, restricted routes or recently landscaped sections are identified before the installation is planned.

Setting Out And Excavation

The footprint is marked out and the area excavated to remove unsuitable surface material and create the space required for the prepared base and concrete.

Sub-Base Preparation

Where required, MOT Type 1 is installed and compacted beneath the base. The preparation is carried out according to the ground found on site and the requirements of the intended structure.

Formwork And Reinforcement

Timber formwork is positioned to the required dimensions. Reinforcement is added where the project requires it before the concrete is placed.

Concrete Placement And Finishing

Fresh concrete is brought to the prepared area, placed and levelled to produce the finished base. The installation is checked before the work is completed.

A Base Designed Around The Actual Site

The dimensions and position of a concrete base need to work with both the garden and the building being installed. On newer Stony Stratford properties, that can mean working within a defined garden layout while dealing with limited access from the side or rear of the house.

Getting The Preparation Right From The Start

A base can look straightforward once the concrete is down, but the preparation underneath it is what needs to be dealt with properly. Poor sub-base preparation can contribute to sinking or movement later, which is why the ground is assessed before the concrete is placed.
