Concrete Base Installation In Whitehouse Gardens

Many of the properties around Whitehouse are modern homes on newer developments, with gardens that have been landscaped as part of the wider build. When a homeowner later wants a concrete base installed, the garden may already have paving, fencing, turf or other finished areas in place.

We look at the proposed position of the base and the route needed to reach it before the installation is planned. On newer estates, side access can be limited, and the most direct route into the garden is not always suitable for moving materials and excavation spoil.

The ground also needs to be checked rather than assuming that a recently finished garden is ready for a new foundation. Areas that have been landscaped can need further preparation before the concrete is placed.

Proper Ground Preparation Comes First

The finished concrete is only as good as the preparation underneath it. On Whitehouse properties, we assess the ground where the base is going rather than simply working from the appearance of the existing garden.

Newly Landscaped Ground

New gardens can contain areas of recently placed soil, turf or landscaping material that have not settled evenly. The installation area may therefore need to be excavated and prepared before the sub-base is placed.

Working Around New-Build Access

Modern housing estates can have limited entry points into rear gardens. We consider the route from the access point to the proposed base before work begins, including how excavation material will leave the garden and how the concrete will reach the prepared area.

A Concrete Base Planned Around The Whitehouse Property

The garden layout can make a noticeable difference to how a base installation is carried out. A modern property may have a narrow side passage, fencing close to the house or a finished garden that needs to be protected while the groundwork is completed.

We plan the installation around the actual site rather than assuming every Whitehouse property has the same access. The proposed footprint, existing ground and route into the garden all form part of the assessment.

This helps keep the job organised from excavation through to concrete placement and reduces unnecessary disruption around the rest of the property.

Our Whitehouse Concrete Base Installation Process

We keep the installation straightforward. The exact preparation depends on the ground, dimensions of the base and access available at the property.

Initial Site Assessment

We discuss the intended building, proposed base dimensions and position. Access into the garden is considered at the same time so the installation can be organised around the property.

Setting Out And Excavation

The base area is measured and marked out before unsuitable material is excavated. Where recently landscaped ground is involved, the condition of the underlying area is considered as part of the preparation.

Sub-Base Preparation

A suitable MOT Type 1 sub-base is installed and compacted where required. The preparation underneath the concrete is an important part of preventing later movement or sinking.

Formwork And Reinforcement

Formwork is set out to the required dimensions. Reinforcement is included where the requirements of the project call for it.

Concrete Placement And Finishing

Fresh concrete is placed, levelled and finished to provide the surface required for the intended building. The installation is checked before the work area is completed.

Keeping The Installation Organised From Start To Finish

On newer Whitehouse properties, keeping the work organised matters because finished gardens and restricted access can leave less room for moving materials around the site.

We consider the access route, excavation and concrete delivery before the installation begins. This means the practical side of the job is planned alongside the base itself rather than being dealt with once work is already underway.

Built Around The Intended Building

The dimensions and position of the concrete base need to work with the building that will sit on it. We take the proposed footprint and available garden space into account when discussing the installation.
