Concrete Bases Built Around The Way Wolverton Properties Work

Many Wolverton gardens sit within established residential layouts where space has to be used carefully. A new outdoor building may need to be positioned around existing paths, boundaries, neighbouring properties or a restricted route into the garden.

That is why the installation needs to be considered as a complete job rather than simply measuring out an area and ordering concrete. We look at where the base is going, how the ground will be prepared and how the materials can be moved into position without creating unnecessary problems around the property.

Where an existing base is being replaced, the condition of the old surface and the ground beneath it also need to be assessed. Where a completely new base is being installed, we start by preparing the area for the requirements of the intended building.

Our Wolverton Concrete Base Installation Process

We keep the process straightforward, but the exact work depends on the size of the base, the condition of the ground and the access available at the property.

Initial Site Assessment

We discuss the intended building, the proposed position of the base and the route available for access. This allows the installation to be planned around the actual Wolverton property rather than a standard set of assumptions.

Excavation

The installation area is excavated to remove unsuitable surface material and create the required space for the prepared sub-base and concrete.

Sub-Base Preparation

A suitable MOT Type 1 sub-base is installed and compacted beneath the concrete. The amount of preparation required depends on the existing ground and the demands of the finished building.

Formwork And Reinforcement

Formwork is set out to the required dimensions. Where the project calls for it, reinforcement is positioned before the concrete is placed.

Concrete Placement And Finishing

The concrete is placed, levelled and finished to provide the surface required for the intended building. The installation is then checked before the site is handed back.

The Base Needs To Work With The Building And The Site

The size and position of the concrete base need to match the building being installed and the space available in the garden. On a Wolverton property with tighter access, the installation also needs to be organised around how the materials will reach the working area.

Avoiding Problems Before The Pour

Problems with access, ground levels or the proposed position are easier to deal with before excavation begins. We therefore look at the job as a whole before the installation starts, including the route into the garden and the work needed to prepare the base correctly.

If you already have measurements or plans for the building, send them over when you get in touch. We can discuss the proposed size, position and access before arranging the next step.

Common Problems We See With Garden Base Projects

A concrete base can look straightforward from the finished surface, but problems often begin before the concrete arrives.

Poorly Considered Access

On properties with narrow side access, the route into the garden needs to be considered before materials and equipment are ordered. Leaving this until the day of the work can create avoidable delays.

The Base Position Has Not Been Properly Planned

The position of the base needs to work with the intended building and the rest of the garden. A small change in position can affect access, boundaries and how the building will be used.

The Ground Beneath The Slab Is Not Properly Prepared

The concrete is only one part of the installation. The area beneath it needs to be prepared around the conditions found on the site and the requirements of the building going on top.

If you are unsure whether your proposed location will work, get in touch before starting the groundwork. We can discuss the project and the access available at your Wolverton property.
