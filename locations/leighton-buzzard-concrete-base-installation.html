Concrete Base Installation Leighton Buzzard

A lot of the properties we work around Leighton Buzzard are established family homes with gardens that have changed over the years. Old paving, partial concrete bases, garden walls and different levels can all affect where a new base can go and how the ground needs to be prepared.

Before starting, we look at the proposed position of the base, what is already underneath the area and how materials will reach the garden. The base needs to suit both the ground available and the building that will sit on it.

Where an existing slab or old base is in the way, this is dealt with as part of the preparation rather than building over unsuitable ground. Getting the preparation right at this stage helps avoid problems such as movement or sinking later on.

Concrete Base Work Planned Around The Property

Garden access is one of the first practical things we consider on a Leighton Buzzard installation. The available route from the front or side of the property can determine how excavation spoil is removed and how the prepared materials and concrete reach the work area.

Established Residential Gardens

Older residential properties can have established gardens with fences, steps and raised sections that were not designed around bringing groundwork equipment into the rear garden. We assess the route before the work starts and organise the installation around what is actually available.

Existing Ground And Old Bases

We regularly come across gardens where old slabs or parts of previous bases are still present. These need to be considered before excavation and preparation begins so the new concrete is not simply placed onto ground that has not been properly dealt with.

A Properly Prepared Base For The Intended Building

The finished base needs to work with the building that is going onto it. That means considering the footprint, position and surrounding garden before the concrete is ordered and the pour is arranged.

We set out the proposed area, deal with the existing ground and prepare the base beneath the concrete according to the requirements of the job. Where reinforcement is appropriate, this is included as part of the installation rather than treated as an afterthought.

The aim is straightforward: prepare the ground properly and produce the concrete base the project actually requires.

Why Proper Ground Preparation Matters

Most problems with a concrete base are difficult to correct once the building has been installed. If the ground underneath has not been prepared properly, movement or sinking can affect everything above it.

That is why we do not rush the preparation stage. Existing slabs, unsuitable material and changes in ground level are dealt with before the concrete is placed. The access route is also considered from the start so the installation does not become unnecessarily complicated once work begins.

  • Ground assessed before installation
  • Existing slabs and old bases considered
  • Garden access planned before work starts
  • Base set out around the intended building
  • Suitable sub-base preparation where required
  • Reinforcement used where the project requires it

Our Leighton Buzzard Concrete Base Installation Process

We keep the installation straightforward and organise the work around the actual property rather than assuming every garden can be approached in the same way.

Site And Access Assessment

We discuss the intended building and look at the proposed base position. Steps, fences, raised sections, narrow routes and existing garden surfaces are considered before excavation begins.

Setting Out And Excavation

The base area is marked out and the existing surface and unsuitable material are removed as required. Where an old slab or partial base is present, its condition is considered before the new preparation is carried out.

Sub-Base Preparation

A suitable MOT Type 1 sub-base is installed and compacted where required to prepare the ground beneath the concrete. The amount of preparation depends on the existing ground and the requirements of the project.

Formwork And Reinforcement

Formwork is set to the required dimensions and reinforcement is positioned where the project calls for it.

Concrete Placement And Finishing

Fresh concrete is placed, levelled and finished to provide the surface required for the intended building. The installation is checked before the work is completed.

Handling Difficult Garden Access Without Overcomplicating The Job

Some Leighton Buzzard gardens have a clear route into the rear of the property. Others involve steps, fences or raised sections that make the route less direct.

These conditions affect how spoil is removed, how materials are moved and how the concrete placement is organised. We look at those details before the installation rather than trying to work them out once the job is already underway.

If access is restricted, tell us when you make contact. A few details about the garden and the proposed position of the base can help us understand what will be involved.
