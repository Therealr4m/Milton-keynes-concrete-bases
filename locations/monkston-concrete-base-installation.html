Monkston Garden Concrete Base Installation

Our Monkston concrete base work starts with looking at the actual garden rather than assuming every installation can be approached in the same way. In established residential properties, the proposed area can already contain old slabs, sections of previous bases or other ground that needs to be dealt with before a new base is installed.

The position of the base also needs to work with the intended building and the space available around it. We look at the footprint, existing ground and route into the garden before deciding how the installation should be organised.

Where access is straightforward, the job can be planned around a clear route into the garden. Where there are steps, fences or raised sections, those restrictions are considered before excavation and concrete delivery are arranged.

Preparing The Base Around The Actual Monkston Property

A lot of the practical work happens before the concrete arrives. The existing surface needs to be understood, the installation area prepared and the route into the garden considered so the job can be carried out without unnecessary disruption.

Established Garden Layouts

Many Monkston properties have established gardens where paving, fencing and previous landscaping have been in place for years. If old slabs or part of an existing base are in the proposed area, these can affect how the ground is prepared for the new installation.

Access And Material Planning

Steps, fences and raised sections can change how excavation spoil, aggregates and concrete need to reach the work area. We assess the available route before the installation so the practical side of the job is planned around the property rather than discovered once work has started.

A Concrete Base Designed Around The Monkston Site

There is no single approach that suits every garden. One Monkston property may have a clear side route into the rear garden, while another may have steps, fencing or a raised section that makes the working route more involved.

The ground itself can also vary. Gardens with old slabs or sections of previous construction may require more preparation before the new base can be formed. We take those conditions into account when planning the installation.

The aim is to prepare the area properly for the intended building and organise the work so the installation fits the property rather than forcing the property to fit a standard process.

Why Preparation Matters On Monkston Concrete Bases

One of the problems with rushing a garden base is what happens underneath it. If the sub-base is not prepared properly for the ground conditions, movement or sinking can become an issue later.

That is particularly important when an existing garden has old paving, sections of previous concrete or ground that has changed over time. The area needs to be cleared and prepared according to what is actually found on site.

We organise the installation around those conditions from the start. This helps keep the work controlled and reduces the chance of having to deal with avoidable ground problems after the concrete has been installed.

Our Monkston Concrete Base Installation Process

We keep the installation process straightforward, with the work planned around the proposed base, the existing ground and the access available to the property.

Site Assessment

We discuss the intended building and proposed base dimensions before looking at the garden. Access, existing surfaces, steps, fences and any raised sections are considered at this stage.

Setting Out And Excavation

The base area is marked out and the existing surface and unsuitable material are removed to create the space required for the installation.

Sub-Base Preparation

Where required, MOT Type 1 is installed and compacted to prepare the ground beneath the concrete. The preparation is based on the actual condition of the site rather than treating every garden identically.

Formwork And Reinforcement

Formwork is set out to the required dimensions and reinforcement is installed where the project requires it before the concrete is placed.

Concrete Placement And Finishing

The concrete is placed, levelled and finished to provide the required surface for the intended building. The installation is checked before the work area is completed.

The Base Needs To Work With What You Are Building

The concrete base needs to be planned around the footprint and position of the intended building. That means considering the available garden space, the ground beneath the installation and how the structure will sit once the work is complete.

Planning The Installation Before Concrete Arrives

Getting the preparation and access arrangements right beforehand keeps the installation organised when the concrete is delivered. This is especially useful where the route into an established Monkston garden includes steps, fences or other restrictions.
