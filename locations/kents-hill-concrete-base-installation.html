Kents Hill Concrete Base Installations

Our team at Concrete Base Milton Keynes regularly works on residential properties where the proposed base needs to be fitted into an established garden rather than treated as an isolated slab.

Many Kents Hill properties have detached family homes with established rear gardens. That gives homeowners good space for an outdoor building, but the practical route from the front of the property to the rear is not always straightforward.

Narrow side pathways, fencing, paving and existing garden features can all affect how excavation spoil, sub-base material and concrete are moved into position. We look at those points before the work starts so the installation can be organised around the property.

The ground itself also matters. Some garden areas can have softer ground beneath the surface, so the base preparation needs to be considered properly rather than simply placing concrete over whatever is already there.

Concrete Base Work Planned Around The Property

On residential jobs in Kents Hill, access is one of the practical details we establish before excavation begins. A garden may look open once you are standing at the rear of the house, while the route to reach it can be much tighter.

Narrow Side Access

Where a narrow pathway runs alongside the house, the installation has to be organised around the space available. Materials and removed ground need to be handled without assuming that machinery or a large vehicle can simply reach the rear garden.

Working Around Established Gardens

Kents Hill has plenty of established residential gardens where fencing, paving and landscaping are already in place. We work out the practical route into the proposed base area before starting, including what needs to be removed or protected during the installation.

Ground Preparation Comes First

If the existing ground is soft or unsuitable, it needs to be dealt with before the concrete is placed. The prepared sub-base provides the support beneath the concrete rather than relying on the existing garden ground to carry the load.

A Concrete Base Designed Around What You Are Building

The base needs to suit the footprint and position of the intended building as well as the garden itself. We establish the dimensions and proposed location before setting out the work so there is a clear connection between the foundation and what is going on top.

This is particularly important on established Kents Hill properties where there may be existing paving, fencing or other garden features close to the proposed installation area.

The aim is not simply to leave a finished-looking slab. The ground beneath it needs to be prepared for the use of the base so that movement caused by poor preparation is less likely to create problems for the building later.

Why Proper Preparation Matters On Kents Hill Base Work

A concrete base can look finished once the surface has been levelled, but the work underneath it is what supports the installation. If unsuitable ground is left beneath the base or the preparation is not appropriate for the site, movement can become a problem later.

We therefore deal with the ground before concentrating on the finished concrete. The existing surface is assessed, unsuitable material is removed where necessary and the sub-base is prepared and compacted before the concrete is placed.

  • Sub-base preparation suited to the site
  • Base dimensions set around the intended building
  • Access planned before materials arrive
  • Work organised around established gardens
  • Installation handled from excavation through to finish

Our Kents Hill Concrete Base Installation Process

We keep the installation straightforward and organise the work around the actual property. The exact approach depends on the size of the base, the condition of the existing ground and how easily the rear garden can be reached.

Site Assessment And Access Planning

We discuss the proposed building and base dimensions, inspect the area and look at the route from the property to the installation point. Narrow pathways and restricted side access are considered before the job is organised.

Setting Out And Excavation

The base area is marked out and the ground is excavated to create the space required for the prepared foundation and concrete. Existing surfaces or unsuitable material are dealt with as required.

Sub-Base Preparation

Where the ground conditions require it, MOT Type 1 is installed and compacted to form the prepared layer beneath the concrete. This is particularly relevant where the existing garden ground is soft or not suitable to support the finished base directly.

Formwork And Reinforcement

Formwork is positioned to the required dimensions and checked before the concrete is placed. Reinforcement is included where the requirements of the particular project call for it.

Concrete Placement And Finishing

The concrete is brought to the prepared area, placed and levelled to provide the required finished surface. The installation is then checked before the site is left ready for the next stage of the project.

Working Efficiently Around Kents Hill Homes

Most residential concrete base work involves more than the concrete itself. The job has to be coordinated around the house, the garden, the access route and the stage the wider project is at.

Restricted Access Does Not Mean The Site Cannot Be Prepared Properly

A narrow side pathway can change how the job is organised, particularly where the proposed base is at the back of an established garden. We consider the route for excavation material and incoming materials before the work begins rather than trying to solve access issues once the concrete is already booked.

The Existing Garden Is Part Of The Job

On active family residential properties, the garden is often already being used and landscaped. The working area needs to be planned so excavation and concrete placement can take place without treating the rest of the garden as an afterthought.

For homeowners looking for the wider service, our Milton Keynes concrete base installation service covers residential base work across the surrounding area.

Common Problems We Avoid By Preparing The Base Properly

One of the problems with rushing a garden base is that issues can appear after the building has already been installed. If the ground beneath the concrete is not suitable, movement can affect the base and potentially cause problems with the structure sitting above it.

This is why we do not treat excavation and sub-base preparation as minor parts of the job. They are part of producing the foundation the project actually needs.

We also avoid making assumptions about access. In Kents Hill, the distance from the road to the rear garden can be very different from one property to another. Checking the route beforehand helps the installation run more efficiently when the work starts.
